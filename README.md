# SaaS FP&A Financial Model

[![CI](https://github.com/rxj0102/saas-fpa-financial-model/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/rxj0102/saas-fpa-financial-model/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

A from-scratch FP&A toolkit for a hypothetical early-stage SaaS company:
synthetic operating data, a linked three-statement model, core SaaS KPIs, a
bottoms-up annual budget, budget-vs-actual variance analysis, and a rolling
12-month cash flow forecast with scenario planning — exported to a
management-reporting Excel workbook and walked through end to end in a
Jupyter notebook.

**⚠️ Disclaimer:** every number in this repository is **synthetic**,
generated by `src/saas_fpa/data_generator.py` from the driver assumptions in
`src/saas_fpa/assumptions.py`. It does not describe any real company. It was
built to be internally consistent, tie out as a real three-statement model
would, and be directionally realistic for an early-stage, SMB
self-serve + inside-sales SaaS business — for illustration only.

## What FP&A does

Financial Planning & Analysis (FP&A) is the function inside a company that
turns the operating plan into numbers, and the numbers back into decisions:
building the budget, forecasting where the business is headed, explaining
*why* actuals diverged from plan, and giving leadership the metrics and
scenarios they need to make calls on hiring, spend, and fundraising before
those calls become urgent.

## What this project demonstrates

1. **A linked three-statement model** (Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash
   Flow) driven by operating assumptions — headcount plan, churn, CAC,
   sales cycle — rather than hardcoded numbers. The balance sheet balances
   and the cash flow statement reconciles to the change in cash in every
   one of 36 months, by construction (cash flow is built via the indirect
   method directly from net income and balance-sheet deltas).
2. **Core SaaS KPIs**, computed with the exact definitions FP&A teams
   actually use (see below), not a "close enough" approximation.
3. **A bottoms-up Year 3 budget**, built by re-running the same driver-based
   simulation engine forward from the real month-24 exit point using next
   year's operating plan — not a straight line through last year's number.
4. **Budget-vs-actual variance analysis**: a noisy "actual" simulation
   (random driver noise plus two deliberate, named operating events) is
   compared line-by-line against the budget, with a two-threshold
   materiality test and driver categorization (volume / price-rate / timing)
   for every material variance.
5. **A rolling 12-month cash flow forecast** with base/upside/downside
   scenarios that flex growth and churn assumptions, re-branched from a new
   as-of point as new actuals come in.
6. **An Excel workbook** built with formula-driven summary tables (XLOOKUP,
   live arithmetic) and native charts — not static pasted values (see the
   note on PivotTables below).

## SaaS metrics, defined

| Metric | Definition | Why FP&A cares |
|---|---|---|
| **MRR / ARR** | Monthly / annual recurring revenue | The core top-line number for a subscription business |
| **NRR** (Net Revenue Retention) | Of the MRR held by customers who were *already* customers at the start of the period, what fraction remains after churn, contraction, **and expansion** — excludes new-logo bookings | The single best signal of product-market fit; above 100% means the business grows even with zero new sales |
| **GRR** (Gross Revenue Retention) | Same base as NRR, but excludes expansion, so it's capped at 100% | Isolates how much churn/contraction alone costs, independent of upsell success |
| **CAC** | Fully-loaded: total S&M spend (headcount + programs) ÷ new customers, trailing 3-month | What growth actually costs, not a partial "ad spend only" number |
| **LTV:CAC** | (ARPU × gross margin ÷ monthly churn) ÷ CAC | The standard unit-economics health check; 3:1+ is considered healthy |
| **Gross margin** | (Revenue − COGS) ÷ Revenue | How much of each revenue dollar is left to cover opex and profit |
| **Burn rate / runway** | Net burn = max(0, −operating cash flow), trailing 3-month; runway = cash ÷ net burn | How long the company can operate before running out of cash |
| **Rule of 40** | TTM YoY ARR growth % + TTM free-cash-flow margin % | The classic growth-vs-profitability framework; most early-stage, growth-prioritizing companies fail it, which is normal, not alarming |
| **Magic Number** | (This quarter's revenue − prior quarter's) × 4 ÷ prior quarter's S&M spend | A quick read on sales efficiency |

## Running it

```bash
python3 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -e .
pip install -r requirements.txt   # adds pytest, matplotlib, jupyter for tests/notebook

python scripts/run_pipeline.py           # prints the full report, writes output/saas_fpa_model.xlsx
pytest tests/ -v                          # 27 tests, including 3-statement tie-out checks
jupyter notebook notebooks/01_full_walkthrough.ipynb
```

## The Excel workbook

`output/saas_fpa_model.xlsx` has 8 tabs: `Assumptions`, `Data_Actuals`,
`Data_BudgetY3`, `PL_Actual_vs_Budget`, `KPI_Dashboard`,
`Variance_Waterfall`, `Variance_Detail`, `Cash_Flow_Forecast` — with live
XLOOKUP/arithmetic formulas driving the P&L and KPI tabs, and 6 native Excel
charts (MRR/ARR growth, NRR/GRR trend, CAC vs. LTV:CAC, burn & runway, an
EBITDA bridge waterfall, and the scenario cash forecast).

**A note on PivotTables:** native Excel PivotTable *objects* can't be
reliably generated from scratch by openpyxl or xlsxwriter — support in both
libraries is read-oriented and brittle, not a real create-from-Python
workflow. Instead, this workbook uses formula-driven summary tables
(XLOOKUP/SUMIFS-style live formulas) plus native charts, which is a genuine
Excel deliverable an analyst would actually build — just not literally a
PivotTable object.

## Real output from the last run

(Reproducible via `python scripts/run_pipeline.py` — random components use a
fixed seed.)

- **Scope**: Month 1 → $511K ARR / 102 customers / 10 employees / $2.61M
  cash. Month 36 → $2.46M ARR / 353 customers / 26 employees / $1.68M cash
  (4.81x ARR growth). Three-statement model ties out in all 36 months
  (max balance sheet difference: ~$0.0000000009).
- **KPIs**: NRR improved from 93.3% (month 12, TTM) to 105.2% (month 36);
  GRR from 81.4% to 86.0%; CAC rose from $3,624 to $7,326 (trailing 3-month)
  while LTV:CAC stayed healthy at 6.4x; gross margin improved from 76.6% to
  80.0%; runway stood at 10.3 months as of month 36; Rule of 40 was −40.5%
  (typical for an early-stage, growth-prioritizing company).
- **Year 3 budget**: built bottoms-up from the headcount hiring plan,
  a 3.77%→2.20% new-bookings growth curve, and a 1.06%→0.90% churn curve —
  totaling $2.06M budgeted revenue and $3.81M budgeted opex.
- **Variance analysis**: 120 line items analyzed (10 P&L items × 12 months),
  16 breached materiality (13.3%). Full-year revenue beat budget by 1.3%
  ($26.8K) and opex came in 0.2% under budget. Concrete example: in month
  28, S&M overspent budget by 12.3% funding a deliberate paid-acquisition
  push, and new-bookings ARR beat budget by 29.3% the same month — a
  volume-driven beat that shows the incremental spend converted into real
  pipeline.
- **Cash flow forecast**: as of month 36, all three scenarios (downside,
  base, upside) project cash running out within 8.1–8.7 months without
  additional financing — showing that growth alone doesn't solve the runway
  problem here, since the hiring plan scales with the same ARR milestones
  that drive growth.
- **Code**: 1,659 lines across 8 modules in `src/saas_fpa/` (2,087 lines
  including tests and the pipeline script), 27 passing tests (5 test
  files), including tie-out assertions on the balance sheet and cash flow
  statement.
